Responsible research

This is a mind map of the Research Ethics for Doctoral Students course. The map is organised as a tree, but in reality things are more interwined (something about "research security" can also fall under "legal compliance"). The numbers in parenthesis correspond to a section of the ethics course MOOC. What is not covered by the ethics course can be provided as external links. Research support triage checklist

Twelve questions to ask a researcher. If the answer is yes or unsure, look at the hints and flag the case for the right specialist.

Partners and productsDataPeople and healthTechnology and securityEthics
  1. 1 Partners and products

    Is this basic research, applied research or work with a company?

    What to look for

    Is a company funding the research or involved in decisions? Who owns the results? Will the results be used commercially? More than one category can apply.

    • EU State-aid rules
    • R&D cooperation rules
  2. 2 Data

    Are they using existing healthcare or social welfare data?

    What to look for

    Patient records, hospital data, health registers, Kanta data or biobank samples/data. Ask where the data comes from and whether it was collected for another purpose. Not all health data fall under the Secondary Use Act.

    • Findata – Secondary Use Act
    • EHDS Regulation secondary-use rules mainly from March 2029
  3. 3 Data

    Is there sensitive personal data?

    What to look for

    Health, genetic data, biometrics used to identify people, ethnicity, religion, political opinions, sexual orientation, etc. Criminal records also need special attention.

    • GDPR – Articles 9, 10 and 89
    • Finnish Data Protection Act
  4. 4 Data

    Is there any other personal data?

    What to look for

    Interviews, recordings, photographs, student data, IP addresses, online activity, location data, pseudonymised data. Can a person be identified, even indirectly?

    • GDPR
    • Aalto Data Protection Policy
  5. 5 People and health

    Are people participating in the research? Does any TENK 4.2(a–f) condition apply?

    What to look for

    For non-medical human research, check:

    1. a) no normal informed consent
    2. b) intervention in physical integrity
    3. c) children under 15 without the specified parental consent/information
    4. d) exceptionally strong stimuli
    5. e) risk of unusually serious mental harm
    6. f) safety risks to participants, researchers or people close to them

    Any one may require prior ethics review.

    • TENK guidelines – Section 4.2
    • Aalto Research Ethics Committee
  6. 6 People and health

    Is this medical research, or are they testing something on humans?

    What to look for

    Clinical trials, medicines, medical devices, diagnostic methods, medical interventions, human tissues or samples. Check whether medical ethics review, permissions or other approvals are required.

    • Finnish Medical Research Act
    • EU Clinical Trials Regulation
    • Medical Devices Regulation
  7. 7 Technology and security

    Are they developing or using AI for something that could be high-risk or prohibited?

    What to look for

    AI assessing students, job applicants or employees; biometric identification; emotion recognition; manipulation; surveillance; decisions affecting people's rights or safety. Even in basic research, check whether the scientific-research exclusion applies, particularly for real-world testing or operational use.

    • AI Act – Research exclusions (Art. 2)
    • Prohibited AI (Art. 5)
    • High-risk AI (Annex III)
  8. 8 Technology and security

    Could export controls, sanctions or dual-use rules apply?

    What to look for

    Military or defence applications, encryption, advanced electronics, quantum technology, drones, certain biotechnology or other controlled technologies. Also check foreign collaborators and access to controlled software or technical information from abroad.

    • Aalto – Export control and research security
    • EU Dual-Use Regulation
    • Finnish Ministry for Foreign Affairs
  9. 9 Technology and security

    Are they working with hazardous or otherwise regulated materials or technologies?

    What to look for

    Animal experiments, GMOs, hazardous chemicals, radiation, dangerous biological agents, environmental releases or high-risk laboratory work. Check whether special permissions or safety assessments are needed.

    • Animal research
    • REACH
    • GMO contained use
    • Radiation safety
  10. 10 Data

    Are they sharing data, software or technology with others?

    What to look for

    External companies, cloud services, AI services, foreign partners, shared research infrastructures. Is the data personal, confidential, copyrighted, licensed or export-controlled? Check who receives access and from where.

    • GDPR
    • EU Copyright Directive
    • Trade Secrets Directive
  11. 11 Ethics

    Could the research cause harm or raise other ethical concerns?

    What to look for

    Vulnerable participants, discrimination, surveillance, harmful uses of results, environmental damage, sensitive international research, human embryos, misuse of AI or other technologies. Ethical concerns can arise even when TENK review is not mandatory.

    • European Commission – Ethics Self-Assessment Guide (2026)
    • TENK – Ethical review guidance
  12. 12 Partners and products

    Will the results become a product, service or something used outside the research project?

    What to look for

    Research software given to external users, AI systems making real decisions, medical devices, spin-outs, licensed technologies or commercial products. Legal requirements can change when something leaves the research environment.

    • AI Act
    • Cyber Resilience Act
    • Medical Devices Regulation

Glossary

EU law

Treaty
The EU's founding agreements (TEU, TFEU). All other EU law must be based on them.
Regulation
Applies directly and in the same way in every EU country, with no national law needed. Example: the GDPR.
Directive
Sets a goal that every EU country must reach, but each country writes its own law to get there.
Transposition
Turning a directive into national law. In Finland this is usually a new or amended act (laki).
Decision
Binding, but only on whoever it is addressed to, such as one country or one company.
Recommendation, opinion
Not binding. They say what the EU thinks should be done.
"Act"
Just a name, not a legal type. The AI Act and the Data Act are both regulations. In Finland, an act (laki) is a law passed by Parliament.
Delegated and implementing acts
Rules adopted by the European Commission to fill in the technical details of a regulation or directive.
Guidelines, soft law
Not legally binding, but authorities follow them in practice. Example: guidelines from the European Data Protection Board.

International law

Convention, covenant
A treaty between countries. Binding on the countries that have ratified it.
Ratification
A country's formal agreement to be bound by a treaty. Signing alone is not enough.
Declaration
A statement of shared principles. Not legally binding, though it can be very influential. Examples: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the Declaration of Helsinki.
Council of Europe
Not part of the EU. A separate organisation of 46 countries, behind the European Convention on Human Rights and the Oviedo Convention.
Nagoya Protocol
Rules for using genetic resources (plants, microbes, ...) from another country: you need its permission and must share the benefits fairly.

Research practice

Research ethics vs research integrity
Ethics asks whether the research is right to do, for people, animals and society. Integrity asks whether it is done honestly and carefully.
FFP
Fabrication, falsification, plagiarism: the three most serious forms of research misconduct.
Questionable research practices
Grey-zone behaviour short of misconduct, such as selective reporting or unfair authorship.
Self-regulation
In Finland, the research community itself handles research integrity violations, following TENK's guidelines, rather than courts.
Legal basis (GDPR)
The legal reason that allows you to process personal data. In research it is often public interest, not consent.
Special categories of personal data
Sensitive data that the GDPR protects more strictly: health, genetic and biometric data, ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, and sex life or sexual orientation.
Medical research (Finland)
Under the Medical Research Act (488/1999), research that intervenes in a person's physical or mental integrity, or involves a human embryo or foetus, to learn about health or disease. It needs a favourable opinion from an ethics committee before it starts.
Research permit
Permission from an organisation to do research on its people or data, separate from ethics review. At Aalto, you need one to survey or interview students or staff, use their working time, observe teaching, or use data Aalto controls, such as student records. Teachers studying their own students need both an ethics review and a permit. How to apply at Aalto
Pseudonymised vs anonymised data
Pseudonymised data can still be linked back to a person with a key, so the GDPR still applies. Truly anonymised data cannot, so it does not.
Data controller
Whoever decides why and how personal data is processed. In university research, usually the university.
DPIA
Data protection impact assessment: a required risk analysis before processing personal data that is likely to put people at high risk.
Dual use
Research or technology that can serve both peaceful and harmful (e.g. military) purposes.
Export control
Rules that require a licence before sharing certain technology, software or know-how outside the EU, even by email or in a talk.
Research security
Protecting research from misuse, foreign interference and unwanted transfer of knowledge.
